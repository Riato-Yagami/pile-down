Pile Down: from jam version 1.2 to 2.2

Pile Down has grown quite a bit since its jam release. The idea is still the same: count down the stacks, remember the hidden values, and find the right tile before time runs out. But there is now much more to discover between attempts: permanent progression, unlockable rewards, challenges, and runs you can share.

This devlog covers the additions since version 1.2, through 2.2.

Progress that survives a game over

The new Progression menu brings together your records, achievements, discovered bonuses, special rules, and tile customization. You can revisit what you have encountered and see what is still waiting to be unlocked.

48 achievements reward a range of goals: reaching later rounds, completing sections without mistakes, playing quickly, combining bonuses, and beating challenges. Each unlocks a reward: a font, a palette, or a challenge.

Bonuses keep track of the highest level you have obtained. Unknown rules and bonuses remain hidden until you discover them, and new entries are marked in the menus. At the end of a run, a recap shows what you gained along the way: discoveries, achievements, and unlocked checkpoints.

Checkpoints to start further ahead

You can now unlock permanent starting points every five rounds. To earn a new checkpoint, you must clear the preceding section without losing a life. Checkpoints unlock in order.

Once unlocked, a checkpoint lets you start directly at that difficulty. You rebuild your bonus collection as you play: bonus choices owed from the skipped rounds are gradually offered after your victories.

This gives you a way to practice difficult sections and explore later rounds without always starting from the beginning. Checkpoint records are kept separate from Classic records.

Six unlockable challenges

Challenges change the starting rules and have their own records. They unlock through achievements, and completing them grants access to their Endless variants.

  • Reload Required: hands no longer guarantee a playable card. You have unlimited reloads to find a solution, without resetting the timer.
  • Shared Clock: one time budget for the entire round. When it runs out, the run ends, even if you still have lives left.
  • Conveyor Belt: cards continuously arrive from the right. Grab the ones you need as they pass; letting a card leave the screen does not cost a life.
  • Boss Rush: special rules appear every round, with increasingly demanding combinations.
  • True Colors: values disappear. You have to rely on tile colors to find your way.
  • One Shot: one life, without the usual protective bonuses.

Replay and share a run

Every run now has a seed. The dedicated page in the Challenges menu accepts a number or text to start a reproducible run.

You can also prepare a setup using challenges, bonuses, and rules you have already unlocked. Available bonus levels follow your progression: you cannot select a level you have never obtained.

Replay and Restart preserve your entered seed and its configuration. You can retry the same setup or share it with someone else. For runs started with an entered seed, you can copy it from the pause menu. An option also lets you display the seed on the results screen, including for random runs.

More variety during rounds

Bonuses and Endless Mode were already part of the jam version. They now come with more goals to pursue and more ways to track your progress.

Three new rules join the pool: Shaking Piles, Wavy Baby, and Pixelated. They make the stacks shake, move them in a wave, or turn the board into large blocks of pixels.

Flawless streaks also earn a reward: reach the next bonus selection without making a mistake since the previous one, and you get three choices instead of the usual two. You still pick one. You can also skip a bonus selection.

Make it your own

The active collection now includes 21 fonts and 23 palettes, including the default choices. The rest unlock as you play.

Fonts change the numbers on your tiles, while palettes let you choose their colors. A preview shows the result before you return to the board. Backgrounds have also been reworked, with patterns, transitions, and distortions that react to the game. You can disable the background or its distortion in the options.

More comfortable menus, on screens big and small

The interface has been reworked with dedicated Options, Progression, and Challenges menus, scrollable lists, and filters for unlocked and locked content.

Four display modes are available: Classic, Semi Adaptive, Adaptive, and Adaptive Menus. The last one expands menus to fit the window while keeping the board compact and centered. You can also choose between HD rendering and native pixel art.

Touch navigation, dropdowns, and bonus interactions have received several fixes. A hint arrow demonstrates the first move at the start of a run, then disappears after your first successful placement. Non-interactive announcements can be skipped to get back to playing sooner.

Available in English and French

The game automatically selects a supported language on first launch, and you can change it later in the options.

The French translation covers menus, descriptions, achievements, and special-rule flavor text. The latest adjustments also fix buttons that were too narrow for translated text, pause popups, and resizing in the Progression and Challenges menus.

Finally, the options let you export and import your save, control achievement notifications, and show or hide the total run timer.

Thanks for playing the jam version and reporting the issues you ran into! Feedback is welcome, especially on the challenges, mobile controls, and menu readability. Which challenge is giving you the hardest time?
